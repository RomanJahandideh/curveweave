# Limitations

CurveWeave is a research prototype. What it does not do, or does only approximately:

## Scope of the method

- **Patches must be labelled.** Each patch is given as a cyclic list of 3 or 4 intended curves. CurveWeave does not infer patch topology from an unstructured network; general cycle detection and SharpEdge-style automatic reconstruction are out of scope. Five-sided or larger loops are rejected with a message.
- **Feature labels are inputs.** Sharp and smooth seams are designer labels. CurveWeave does not infer them from geometry.
- **Fixed boundaries.** Fairing moves only interior vertices. Boundary curves are fixed after repair and resampling, so their own noise survives into the surface (the boundary error is about 0.001 × diagonal at default noise).
- **Snapping is a mean.** A junction moves to the mean of its endpoints. There is no global least-squares adjustment of the curves, and no preservation of curve tangents at junctions.
- **One global resolution.** Every curve is sampled with the same N segments, regardless of length. Long and short curves therefore get very different sample spacing, and triangle quality degrades on thin patches. The minimum triangle angle is reported.
- **Uniform Laplacian.** The fairing operator uses uniform umbrella weights on the parameter grid, not cotangent weights. Its result depends on the parameterization and is not an intrinsic thin-plate energy.
- **The seam term is first order in spirit.** $E_{\text{seam}}$ asks the first interior rows on either side to mirror through the seam. That tends toward tangent continuity but does not enforce G1 exactly; a residual normal angle (about 2°) remains.
- **Only one architectural regularity.** Planarity toward a fixed best-fit plane of the boundary is implemented. If the boundary itself is not planar, planarity can only be approached, not reached. There are no symmetry, offset, developability, structural or code-compliance constraints.
- **The flattening is a preview, not a cut pattern.** Stress-minimizing flattening of one patch, with no seams, bending allowances, material model or nesting. Distortion is reported, but nothing guarantees it is acceptable for fabrication.

## Scope of the evaluation

- **Synthetic data only.** Three procedural networks generated by the same author who designed the repair rules. The noise model contains exactly the failure modes the method targets. The benchmark contains no real VR or hand-drawn sketches.
- **No user decisions simulated.** Suggestions count as misses, which understates what a designer would reach interactively. Equally, no user study shows that designers make good decisions with the interface.
- **Tangent scoring not shown to help.** On this benchmark, the precision gain comes from designer labels; tangent and valence evidence alone did not reduce false merges (see [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md)).
- **Simple baseline.** The distance-only baseline does no duplicate removal or fragment joining. A stronger distance-based method would close part of the patch-success gap.
- **No comparison with published systems.** CurveWeave is not compared with SharpEdge or other published reconstruction methods. No claim of improvement over any published method is made.
- **Runtime figures are from one machine** (Node 22 on Windows 11). The browser runs the same code single-threaded on the main thread.

## Engineering

- The whole pipeline reruns synchronously on each change. That is fine for the presets (about 100 ms) but would need a web worker and incremental updates for networks with thousands of curves.
- Imported JSON is validated for structure only. Invalid patch references fail at patch construction, with a message.
- The GLB exporter writes positions and indices only (no normals or materials). OBJ groups faces per patch.
